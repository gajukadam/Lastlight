Plants in danger

Plants are the base of every habitat, and many are disappearing quietly.

How we can protect them

Conservation works when governments, communities and individuals act together.

Protect habitats

Support national parks and sanctuaries. Plant native trees and avoid products linked to deforestation.

Stop wildlife trade

Never buy ivory, skins, exotic pets or illegal timber. Report poaching to forest authorities.

Reduce plastic

Plastic chokes turtles, birds and fish. Carry a bag and bottle; join clean-up drives.

Support research

Donate to or volunteer with trusted groups such as WWF, WII and local NGOs.

Spread awareness

Talk about endangered species at school, college and online.

Fight climate change

Save energy, use public transport and choose sustainable food.

Grow native plants

Home gardens with native species feed pollinators and birds.

Be a responsible tourist

Stay on trails, keep distance from wildlife and never feed animals.

What is the Red Data Book?

A public record of species at risk of extinction, started by the IUCN in 1964.

CategoryMeaning
Vulnerable (VU)High risk of extinction in the wild
Endangered (EN)Very high risk of extinction in the wild
Critically Endangered (CR)Extremely high risk of extinction in the wild
Extinct in the Wild (EW)Survives only in captivity or cultivation

The data helps governments make laws, protect habitats and plan breeding programmes. Status changes as new assessments are published, so always check the official IUCN Red List for the latest.
